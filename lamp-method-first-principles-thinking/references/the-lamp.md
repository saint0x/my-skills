# The LAMP Method

## A Methodology for First-Principles Thinking

## Purpose

This document describes a mode of thinking for discovering better abstractions, perspectives, and solutions.

It is not specific to engineering, science, entrepreneurship, or any one discipline. It is meant for complex systems where conventional assumptions can hide useful structure.

Use it to separate:

- reality from interpretation
- evidence from inherited practice
- claims from conclusions
- local fixes from deeper abstractions

The domain changes. The thinking does not.

## The First Principle

Everything humans build exists in two layers:

- reality
- interpretation

Reality contains things such as physics, mathematics, information, energy, time, probability, and causality.

Interpretation contains things such as conventions, institutions, software, economics, organizations, traditions, best practices, accepted wisdom, and historical accidents.

Never confuse the second for the first. Many constraints people experience are interpretations rather than physical necessities.

First-principles thinking separates the two.

## Reality Is The Only Authority

Accept physical reality. Question everything else.

This does not mean rejecting expertise. It means refusing to confuse consensus with proof.

When someone says, "That cannot be done," ask:

- According to physics?
- According to mathematics?
- According to current evidence?
- Or according to current practice?

Those are different claims.

## The Lamp

Brilliant ideas rarely appear fully formed. They often begin as small observations.

These observations are lamps.

The lamp is not the discovery. It illuminates previously unseen territory.

Most people notice the lamp and stop. The method is to keep walking.

Every important discovery can become the lamp that reveals another. The process does not need to end at the first answer.

## Chase Contradictions

Do not only search for answers. Search for contradictions.

Whenever reality and expectation disagree, stop. That disagreement is valuable because it reveals hidden assumptions.

Ask:

- What assumption must be false for this contradiction to exist?
- Which part of the current explanation fails?
- Is the contradiction caused by evidence, interpretation, measurement, incentives, or language?

Most discoveries begin there.

## Decompose Everything

Large ideas should be decomposed.

Do not ask, "Is this idea correct?" as a single question.

Ask:

- Which pieces are correct?
- Which pieces are incorrect?
- Which pieces are unknown?
- Which pieces are useful even if the original framing is wrong?

Almost every idea contains a mixture of signal and noise. Extract the signal. Discard the noise.

## Separate Claims

Every proposal contains smaller claims. Treat them independently.

For example, instead of evaluating "This impossible machine changes everything" as one statement, break it apart:

- Does it violate physical law?
- Does it require unknown materials?
- Does it require unrealistic economics?
- Does it rely on a false market assumption?
- Does it contain one useful insight hidden among incorrect assumptions?

Often the useful insight matters more than the original proposal.

## Search Outside The Distribution

Novel ideas rarely originate entirely inside mainstream thinking.

Deliberately inspect ideas that are unusual, unconventional, unfashionable, incomplete, poorly articulated, speculative, or outside the dominant frame.

Do not accept them uncritically. Do not dismiss them reflexively. Disassemble them.

Ask:

- What percentage of value exists here?
- Which claim survives contact with evidence?
- What does this framing see that conventional framing misses?
- What does conventional framing correctly reject?

Sometimes the answer is zero. Sometimes the answer is five percent. Occasionally that five percent becomes important.

The goal is not to believe unusual ideas. The goal is to recover valuable structure when it exists.

## Stay Grounded

Imagination without reality becomes fantasy. Reality without imagination becomes incrementalism.

Operate between the two.

Every investigation should include two simultaneous activities:

- Search what is currently known through appropriate sources: web search, literature search, official documentation, datasets, local files, historical examples, expert material, or user-provided context.
- Deliberately search beyond the current distribution by asking which assumptions have become invisible because everyone shares them.

Ground conclusions in evidence whenever possible. Use imagination to generate hypotheses. Use experiments and evidence to eliminate them.

## The Living System

Never study an isolated object when the real behavior belongs to a system.

Everything exists within relationships. Changing one assumption often changes surrounding assumptions.

When one discovery appears, ask:

- What else must change if this is true?
- Which incentives, constraints, interfaces, or feedback loops move with it?
- Does the local observation imply a larger system-level pattern?

Often the real discovery emerges from interactions.

## Follow The Gradient

Every successful observation should generate another question.

Ask:

- If this is true, what larger truth does it imply?
- What would be easier, cheaper, stranger, or more obvious in a world where this is true?
- What adjacent experiment becomes newly worth running?

Do not stop after solving the local problem. Use every answer as the beginning of another investigation.

## Search For Hidden Symmetry

When two things appear identical, test whether they actually are.

When two things appear different, ask whether they are expressions of the same underlying principle.

Useful discoveries often come from hidden asymmetries and unexpected unifications.

## Change Coordinate Systems

Many difficult problems become easier when represented differently.

Before searching for a better answer, search for:

- a better question
- a better representation
- a better coordinate system
- a better unit of analysis
- a better timescale

Sometimes the discovery is not the solution. It is the language that makes the solution obvious.

## Think Across Domains

Most revolutionary ideas involve transfer.

A structure discovered in one field may already solve a problem in another.

Ask:

- What else behaves like this?
- Where has this pattern appeared before?
- Which discipline would find this problem ordinary?

The analogy is not the discovery. It is another lamp.

## Build Small Experiments

Do not rely on intuition alone.

Every interesting hypothesis should become the smallest experiment capable of disproving it.

Design experiments that attempt to destroy the idea. Ideas that survive repeated attempts to falsify them become more valuable.

Reality is the final judge.

## Prefer Questions Over Conclusions

Conclusions can end thinking. Questions extend it.

Every answer should produce better questions.

The objective is not premature certainty. The objective is progressively improving understanding.

## Think In Layers

Every system exists at multiple scales.

Move between:

- details
- patterns
- systems
- abstractions

Repeat until they align. The microscopic explanation and macroscopic explanation should eventually make sense together.

## Beware Invisible Assumptions

The most dangerous assumptions are the ones that feel obvious.

Whenever something feels obvious, ask:

- When did I learn this?
- What evidence supports it?
- Would someone from another discipline make the same assumption?
- Would this still be true under different technology, incentives, constraints, or scale?

Many breakthroughs occur by questioning ideas everyone stopped noticing.

## Curiosity Before Ego

Do not become attached to ideas. Become attached to discovering what is true.

Treat invalidation as information. Reality has taught you something.

The fastest learners are willing to test their favorite ideas first.

## The North Star

The objective is not merely to solve problems.

The objective is to discover better ways of thinking about problems.

A good solution improves one system. A better abstraction improves every future solution.

Seek abstractions. Seek hidden structure. Seek deeper questions.

Every surprising observation is a lamp.

Pick it up. Walk further. Repeat.
