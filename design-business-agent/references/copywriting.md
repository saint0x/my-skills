# Copy Is Part of the Interface

Treat copywriting as design. Research the client's category and customer before constructing messaging.

Prefer:

- specificity over abstraction
- clarity over cleverness
- benefits over feature inventories
- evidence over adjectives
- concrete outcomes over corporate language

The hero should rapidly answer some combination of:

1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. Why should I care?
4. Why should I believe you?
5. What should I do next?

The exact hierarchy depends on the visitor's existing awareness. Do not force every business into the same landing-page template.

## The Sharp-Copy Rule

Masterful copy is not more words — it's fewer, harder ones. The load-bearing line in any section (headline, subhead, CTA microcopy) should land in **one or two sentences maximum**, and should convey whatever tone the moment needs — confident, urgent, reassuring, playful — in the fewest possible words that still do the job.

If a line can be cut in half without losing the point, cut it. If a sentence is doing decorative work rather than persuasive or clarifying work, delete it. Long paragraphs of marketing copy are a tell of weak positioning, not thoroughness — if the proposition needs three sentences to land, the proposition isn't sharp enough yet, not the copy.

Body/support copy can run longer where genuine detail or proof is needed (specs, process, FAQ), but the primary hierarchy line of any section is the short, hard-hitting one.
